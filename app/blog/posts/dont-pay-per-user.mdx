I've sold business software for more than two years. Two things slowed down almost every deal: onboarding that was hard to explain, and pricing that was hard to explain. I ran into both myself.

Onboarding is the bigger problem, and it needs time and product work. Pricing is different. Frappe already has the answer. It just isn't saying it clearly.

I know this because I was stuck on it.

Earlier this year, I showed a Frappe HR demo to the head of sales at the company I worked for. He loved it. His first question was: how much will it cost?

I told him Frappe Cloud pricing is compute-based, not per-user. That was true, and it didn't help. He needed a number he could put in front of a client. So did I. I wrote to Frappe asking how to explain the pricing in numbers to a business that only knows its headcount.

That email is part of how I ended up working with Frappe. And the question in it is still the question most buyers ask.

Frappe doesn't charge per user.

Most business software does. One person, one license. It sounds fair, but watch what it does to a growing company.

Every new hire becomes a line item. Teams share one login to save money. The accountant gets access, the storekeeper doesn't. On the shop floor, people write on paper and someone types it in later. Not because they can't use software. Because every login costs money.

Per-user pricing quietly decides who in a company gets to work with good data.

Frappe works differently. ERPNext, CRM, Helpdesk and HR are open source. A business can run them on its own server and pay no license fee at all. On Frappe Cloud, the bill follows the compute the business uses, not the number of people who log in.

To be precise about it: the bill does change. When a business does more work, it moves up a plan. What never happens is paying extra because one more person needs a login.

So why don't more people know this?

When Apple launched the iPod, it didn't say "5 GB storage." It said 1,000 songs in your pocket. The first is accurate. The second is understood.

Frappe calls its model compute-based pricing. That's accurate. But a business owner comparing ERPs doesn't think in compute. He thinks in people. "Add your whole team. No per-user fee." is the version he understands.

This isn't a proposal to change Frappe's pricing. The pricing stays exactly the same. The iPod held the same 5 GB whether Apple called it 5 GB or 1,000 songs. What changes is how it's said.

That gap between what's true and what's understood is what I want to close.

I shared this idea inside Frappe. The response was simple: if you believe it, show that it works.

So starting today, I'm writing a series on LinkedIn called "Don't pay per user." Ten posts over five weeks, twice a week, all on one idea. A few rules I've set for myself:

- Every line has to be true for every customer on every plan.
- No prices, no numbers that can't be checked.
- No attacking other products.

I'll track what happens against my own baseline and publish the results in early November, including what didn't work.

If the idea is right, it should show. If it isn't, I'll write about that too.
